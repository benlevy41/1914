# <p align="center">1914</p>
## <p align="center">The Western Front</p>

### Background: 
In August, 1914 the German Empire invaded Belgium, violating Belgian neutrality and drawing France, Russia and the United Kindom into the conflict that would become the first World War.  Widely known for the brutal and mostly static trench warfare, the Western Front initially saw a rapid war of movement as the German army quickly pushed through Belgium and Luxembourg and into northern France. Only German missteps and a few heroic defensive actions on the part of the Allied forces kept the Germans from sweeping through into Paris.  As the guns of August roared, history hung in the balance and no one knew how this war would end...

### Objective:
The object of the game is complete destruction of all enemy forces and capture of their respective centers of gravity: Paris for the Allies, and Koblenz* for the German Empire.

\* *Note that although Koblenz was a strategic HQ for the German military, the capitol of the German Empire was Berlin.  Koblenz was selected as the German COG primarily because Berlin is too far to fit on the game board!*

## Risk-type Game Play Rules:
These rules are reminiscent of the table-top strategy game *Risk* with a few modifications to make gameplay shorter and to emphasize the encirclement strategy which was so central to the Schleiffin Plan.

### Setup:
The game begins with each player assigned a random draw of 15 cities and their respective center of gravity.  Each occupied city has one unit of infantry. Players have five additional infantry units that can be placed on any city occupied by their forces.  These reserve units are placed one at a time by turns, starting with the German Empire, until all troops have been emplaced.  

### Game Play:
Each turn consists of three phases: reinforcement, attack, and reorganization.  At the beginning of the turn, each player is assigned a number of reinforcements based on the number of territories that they hold at the start of the turn.  Every five cities occupied by the player contribute one infantry unit, and no partial units are assigned.  These reinforcements must be placed in cities that are connected to the player's center of gravity by roads that are completely controlled by that player.  Isolated cities cannot be reinforced.  If a player's center of gravity is occupied by the enemy, they will not receive reinforcements for that turn.  All reinforcements must be placed before attacking.  

Players can occupy an abandoned city or attack a city occupied by the enemy that is directly connected to one of their cities by a road.  The player can choose to attack with any number of units up to the number occupying that city.  Combat is decided by rolling dice, with each attacking unit contributing a single roll of a die. The defending player is allowed to commit any number of forces to combat up to the number in the defending garrison, but must commit at least one. The highest rolling dice of both sides are compared and the lower roll loses.  If a roll is equal, the defender gets the advantage.  Each losing roll results in one unit from that player being removed from the field.  An attacking player may choose to attack as long as they have forces to commit.  If during the attacking phase the defenders are eliminated from a garrison, the attacking player must move the number of troops committed to the attack from the attacking garrison into the defending garrison.  If the attacker has only one unit left in the attacking garrison, the player is allowed  to abandon a garrison, leaving it unoccupied. Likewise, if an attacking player loses all units in a garrison, the garrison will remain unoccupied.

After the player is finished attacking, either by choice or because they have no more troops to commit, they begin the reorganization phase.  During reorganization, a player can move units along contiguous roadways that are fully occupied by their forces.  Isolated contiguous sections of roads can also be traversed - they do not have to be connected to the player's center of gravity.  A player may choose to abandon a city, leaving it unoccupied. Unoccupied cities cannot be traversed or occupied during the reorganization phase.  After reorganization is complete, the turn is over and the opposing player starts their turn.

## Advanced Game Play Rules:

The advanced game play rules provide more tactical flexibility and emphasize the importance of key terrain, natural obstacles and avenues of approach. Although the board is the same, each city provides a certain number of troops, roughly corresponding to their prominence as industrial or population centers.  Each route between cities also has an associated size, corresponding to the maximum number of units that are able to attack along its axis.

### Setup:

All cities on the board start out as unoccupied aside from the players' respective centers of gravity and each center of gravity provides 5 units per round.  Each player receives their allotment of 5 units at the beginning of the game and play starts again with the German Empire.

### Game Play:

Each player takes turns and each turn consists of three phases: reinforcement, attack and reorganization.  

#### Reinforcement:

During the reinforcement phase, players receive a number of troops corresponding to the total value of all cities held by that player.  These units can be moved any distance along contiguous roads that are fully controlled by that player by occupied cities on both ends of the road segments. Unoccupied cities may not be traveled through or into during the reinforcement phase.  Reinforcements cannot reach cities that are not connected by contiguous fully controlled roads.  All reinforcements must be made prior to the attack phase.

#### Attack Phase:

After the reinforcement phase, the player enters the attack phase.  During the attack phase the player may choose to attack a city occupied by the opponent or occupy a city that is unoccupied.  An attack or move to occupy a city must originate in a city occupied by the player having at least one unit and that city must be connected to the target city by a single segment of roadway.  

#### Occupying Unoccupied Cities:

The maximum number of troops allowed to occupy an unoccupied city corresponds to the lower of either: the total number in the source city garrison or the number corresponding to the size of the roadway.  Any troops that occupy an unoccupied city may not occupy or attack another city in the same turn.  Once those troops are committed they can no longer be moved until the reorganization phase.

#### Attacking Enemy Cities:

A player can choose to attack an enemy city with any number of troops up to either the number of units in the attacking garrison or the number corresponding to the size of the roadway, whichever is lower; however, the player must commit at least one unit.  The defending player must commit at least one unit, but can commit up to the number of units currently garrisoned in the defending city.  A single 6-sided die is cast for each committed unit on both sides and the highest dice are compared.  The higher rolling die of each pair wins and one unit of the opposite player is removed from the game board.  In the event of a tied pair of dice, the advantage is given to the defender.  Attacks continue until the following:

1. The attacking player ceases the attack - in this case, both players keep the current status quo.
2. The attacking player loses all units in the attacking city garrison - in this case, the city becomes unoccupied.
3. The defending player loses all units in the defending city garrison - in this case, all units committed to the attack move into the target city.  Unlike occupying unoccupied cities, these units are allowed to continue attacking other cities held by the enemy, or they can occupy unoccupied cities contingent on the same restriction on moving after occupying.

#### Reorganization:

After the player is finished attacking, either by choice or because they have no more troops to commit, they begin the reorganization phase.  During reorganization, a player can move units along contiguous roadways that are fully occupied by their forces.  Isolated contiguous sections of roads can also be traversed - they do not have to be connected to the player's center of gravity.  A player may choose to abandon a city, leaving it unoccupied. Unoccupied cities cannot be traversed or occupied during the reorganization phase.  After reorganization is complete, the turn is over and the opposing player starts their turn.

### Note About the Cities:

The cities on the game board were chosen because of their proximity to historic action or their strategic importance during WWI - although the action may have happened after 1914.
